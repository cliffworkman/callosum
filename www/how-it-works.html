How Callosum works

AI can draft. Evidence still has to answer.

Callosum is not a chatbot wrapped around a reference library. It is a local evidence pipeline: retrieval limits what a model sees, generation proposes claims, and independent local systems check those claims against the source.

Explore the pipeline Inspect a saved synthesis
The model does not get a vote on whether its own citations are correct.

Each proposed claim returns to the user with its source passage, page, location precision, and separate retrieval, quotation, and support signals.

Retrieval-augmented generation

RAG starts by narrowing the evidence.

For synthesis, Callosum searches the selected local corpus before generation. The model receives a bounded set of passages, their chunk identities, and the research question. It is not asked to recall the literature from its training data.

The trust spine

Generation and verification are separate jobs.

A fluent sentence is not enough. Every candidate citation passes through local checks whose components remain visible to the reader.

01

Does the cited local passage remain semantically close to the proposed claim?

02

Verbatim evidence

Does the proposed quotation occur in the source text after conservative canonical matching?

03

NLI stance

With the passage as premise and claim as hypothesis, does a local cross-encoder estimate entailment, contradiction, or neutrality?

04

Source location

Can Callosum return the reader to the supporting page and an honestly labelled exact or approximate region?

Different tools earn different claims

Not everything in Callosum is an LLM.

The backend uses several kinds of computation. Their outputs should not share language, colors, or certainty merely because they appear in the same application.

deterministic calculation

Recompute what can be recomputed.

Mathematical and standards-based tools operate without generative AI.

  • statcheck p-value recomputation
  • GRIM, GRIMMER, and DEBIT constraints
  • citation and bibliography rendering
rule + evidence checks

Expose a signal and its basis.

Reporting and provenance checks look for bounded, inspectable evidence rather than issuing judgments about researchers.

  • methods and transparency checklists
  • registration/publication crosswalks
  • reference-integrity signals
local statistical / ML signal

Organize and compare meaning.

Probabilistic local models help rank or classify while keeping their limitations visible.

  • semantic retrieval and axes
  • clustering and tag suggestions
  • NLI stance classification
optional generative assistance

Draft candidates for review.

Models help where language generation reduces labor, but their products stay provisional.

  • synthesis and overview drafting
  • AI-focused triage
  • structured extraction candidates

The honest boundary of automation

Inspectability does not eliminate uncertainty.

Callosum is designed to make failure and uncertainty easier to see—not to make either disappear.

Retrieval can miss.

The strongest passage may fall outside the retrieved set or the selected scope.

Extraction can degrade.

Scans, complex layouts, and malformed PDFs can reduce text or coordinate quality.

NLI is not logical proof.

Entailment and contradiction scores are model estimates that require human interpretation.

Generated prose can mislead.

Fluency does not guarantee completeness, importance, or a defensible scholarly conclusion.

Detectors have bounded targets.

A tool’s silence means only that its specific rule did not produce a signal.

Signals are not accusations.

Methods and integrity tools point the scholar toward evidence to inspect.

Callosum makes the chain inspectable. The researcher remains responsible for the conclusion.
