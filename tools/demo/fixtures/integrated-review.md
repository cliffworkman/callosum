# SYNTHETIC DEMO MANUSCRIPT — NOT A REAL STUDY

## Anomalous-is-bad bias: an integrated evidence review

This deliberately synthetic manuscript exists only to demonstrate Callosum's unpublished-manuscript workspace.
It reports no new participant data and must not be cited as research.

## Abstract

People can draw negative moral and social conclusions from visible facial differences. Workman et al. (2021)
described this anomalous-is-bad stereotype, Rasset et al. (2023) tested gaze and humanity attributions, and
He et al. (2024) tested whether complementary good-is-beautiful and bad-is-ugly stereotypes make moral character
shape perceived facial attractiveness. We performed a synthetic random-effects meta-analysis of Hedges' g for
demonstration only.

## Method

The review protocol was preregistered at https://osf.io/demo-only. Searches and eligibility criteria were defined
before extraction. Two reviewers independently screened records and extracted effect sizes. We used a
random-effects model, reported tau-squared and I-squared heterogeneity, examined influence diagnostics, and
assessed publication bias with a funnel plot and Egger test. This synthetic corpus contains three studies.

We also ran a Bayesian t-test with a Cauchy prior. The synthetic illustration was t(19) = 2.53, BF10 = 500.
Posterior samples came from MCMC; R-hat = 1.21. We report a 95% confidence interval.

## Results

The demonstration includes one intentionally inconsistent frequentist statement: t(18) = 2.10, p = .90.
No numerical result in this synthetic draft is an estimate from the three bundled articles.
